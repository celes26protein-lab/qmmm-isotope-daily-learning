# Step A: N–H vs N–D in N-methylacetamide (NMA)

**Question.** If only the mass of one hydrogen is changed (H → D), which quantities
in a QM/MM calculation change and which do not?

**Short answer from this first pass.** Static quantities from a single point
(energy, Mulliken charges) are identical. A dynamical quantity (the N–H/N–D
stretching frequency in a short QM/MM MD) changes clearly. Mean bond length does
not change measurably. Everything is from **one trajectory with one random seed**.

## System and method

- NMA (`ACE` + `NME`) in a TIP3P water box (`solvatebox ... 10.0`), ff14SB
- QM region: the whole NMA (`qmmask=':1-2'`, 12 atoms, 0 link atoms)
- QM method:PM3
- QM/MM coupling:electrostatic embedding with `qm_ewald=1, qm_pme=1`
- Water molecules treated entirely with MM
- Starting structure: `min_qmmm.rst`, from a PM3 QM/MM minimisation of the 
tleap structure (step 1)
- Starting structure: min_qmmm.rst, obtained from PM3 QM/MM minimisation of the
tleap-generated structure (Step 1)

H → D substitution

The deuterated system was generated by changing the mass of atom 8, 
corresponding to the amide H on the NME residue:

H: 1.008
D: 2.014

## Interpretation

The first-pass result can be summarized conceptually as:

H → D
- **Electronic structure / potential energy surface**
  - Essentially unchanged
- **Static quantities examined here**
  - Energy → unchanged
  - Mulliken charge → unchanged
- **Nuclear dynamics**
  - Stretching frequency → shifted
  - Mean bond length → no measurable shift
          
An isotope substitution can modify nuclear dynamics through the mass while 
leaving the electronic potential surface unchanged.

## Experiment 1: single point, H vs D

`inputs/sp_pm3.in`, same coordinates, same settings, only the topology differs.

| | N–H | N–D |
|---|---|---|
| PM3 ESCF (printed) | -49.8324 | -49.8324 |
| 12 Mulliken charges | identical to 3 decimals | |

Charges (QM numbering = MM numbering here): 1 H 0.091; 2 C -0.144; 3 H 0.058;
4 H 0.039; 5 C 0.250; 6 O -0.390; 7 N -0.062; 8 H 0.065; 9 C -0.076; 10 H 0.084;

This is expected: the electronic energy depends on nuclear *positions*, not masses.
"Identical" means identical to the printed digits; differences below that were not checked.

## Experiment 2: short QM/MM MD, H vs D

`inputs/md_pm3_long.in` was used for a short QM/MM MD simulation:
-QM method: PM3
-1000 steps
-timestep: 0.5 fs
-total simulation time: 0.5 ps
-Langevin thermostat
-temperature: 300 K
-gamma_ln=2.0
-constant volume
-no SHAKE: ntc=1, ntf=1, qmshake=0
-random seed: ig=12345

| | 0.1 ps (200 frames) | 0.5 ps (1000 frames) |
|---|---|---|
| N–H peak (cm⁻¹) | 3336 | 3361 |
| N–D peak (cm⁻¹) | 2335 | 2477 |
| ratio H/D | 1.429 | 1.357 |
| raw frequency bin width (cm⁻¹) | ~330 | ~67 |
| mean N–H / N–D (Å), 0.5 ps | | 1.0010 / 1.0001 |
| std N–H / N–D (Å), 0.5 ps | | 0.0339 / 0.0250 |

The harmonic estimate for an isolated N–H / N–D oscillator from reduced masses is
about 1.37, and the 0.5 ps ratio (1.357) is close to it. **The comparison is weak:**
with a bin width of ~67 cm⁻¹, the ratio is uncertain by several percent, and the
0.1 ps ratio (1.429) shows how much the resolution matters. The analysis script was
checked on synthetic sine waves (input 3360 and 2450 cm⁻¹, recovered 3360.6 and 2451.6).

## What this shows and what it does not

**Shows (for this setup):**
- Energy and charges from a single point do not depend on the isotope
- The vibrational frequency of the substituted bond drops clearly with deuteration
- The mean bond length is practically unchanged

**Does not show:**
- Anything about kinetic isotope effects: nuclei are classical here, so there is
  no zero-point energy and no tunnelling. Those must be added separately (later steps)
- Whether PM3 frequencies agree with experiment or higher-level theory (not compared)
- Whether the difference in N–H and N–D standard deviations (0.034 vs 0.025 Å, which
  was not visible in the 0.1 ps run) is an isotope effect or trajectory-to-trajectory noise.
  One seed only; **replicates with different seeds are the next thing to do**
